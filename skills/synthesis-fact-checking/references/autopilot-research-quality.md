# Research and recommendation review

Start from the decision the research must support. Name the material questions,
which answer would change the recommendation, and the cheapest credible source
or observation that discriminates the alternatives. Search until those decisive
questions are answered to the required standard; neither a fixed citation count
nor unlimited browsing defines completion.

For each decisive claim, retain the exact primary source, relevant passage,
effective date, scope and unresolved limitation. Check whether the passage
supports the claim's actual strength. A quotation proves presence, not entailment.
Look for counterevidence and plausible alternative explanations. Separate a
source's observation, its interpretation, and the recommendation made from it.
A confident source or an independent reviewer can still be wrong.

Calibrate the task's source-verification, decisive-claim and counterevidence
judgments with sound and defective controls. Useful defects include an accurate
quotation attached to a stronger causal claim, a current-looking citation whose
effective date has expired, an omitted decision-changing limitation, or a
recommendation that ignores a contrary primary source. The sound control should
preserve a qualified conclusion when the evidence is incomplete; refusing all
uncertain conclusions is also a review failure.

When a decisive fact has a supported deterministic local observation, record
its question, distinct predictions and consequence through the existing
uncertainty owner. Otherwise keep the question open and attach the appropriate
primary-source or domain evidence. Do not turn an inaccessible source into a
negative finding, or a local fixture into proof of a remote system's state.
Stop investigating when additional information cannot change the declared
decision at the required confidence; state remaining uncertainty explicitly.
