# Meeting prep — factor inventory

The skill weighs every factor below and names the ones that drove the
pack's shape. Factors marked (S) affect sizing; (T) affect tone and
register; (C) affect content selection; (R) affect risk handling.

## 1. The meeting itself

1. Purpose type (S/C): decision, status, brainstorm, negotiation,
   interview, review, retrospective, kickoff, escalation, social.
   A decision meeting needs the decision framed; a status meeting
   needs almost nothing.
2. Recurring vs one-time (S/C): series carry history and open loops;
   one-time meetings need context-setting the series already has.
3. Series health (C): for recurring meetings, what was committed last
   time, what slipped, what pattern the principal should break.
4. Duration (S): prep scales with minutes, sublinearly. A 15-minute
   sync gets five lines; a 90-minute forum gets the full pack.
5. Size (T/C): 1:1, small room (≤5), large room (6–15), forum (15+).
   Candor shrinks as the room grows; attribution discipline rises.
6. Format (C): in-person, remote, hybrid. Hybrid meetings lose the
   remote half first — prep names who is remote and what they need.
7. Time of day and week (T): Monday 9am needs energy and brevity;
   Friday 4pm needs decisions, not new threads.
8. Agenda shared? (C): if yes, prep maps to it item by item; if no,
   prep proposes what the agenda should be and what to ask for.
9. Pre-reads (C): what was sent, whether the principal has read it,
   and the one-line position on each.
10. Meeting owner (C): who called it and why now — the convener's
    motive shapes everything.
11. Principal's role (C): leading, deciding, informing, attending,
    observing. An observer's prep is questions, not talking points.
12. Recorded? (R): if recorded or minuted, every claim must survive
    replay; if unrecorded, candor is possible but capture is manual.

## 2. The participants

13. Familiar vs new faces (C): new people get researched (role,
    background, public writing); familiar people get history.
14. Internal / external / mix (T/R): externals change the
    disclosure boundary completely — see factor 47.
15. Hierarchy map (T/C): boss, peer, report, skip-level, cross-org.
    Each gets its register per R2; mixed rooms write to the least
    technical decision-maker.
16. Technical depth per participant (T): governs ticket numbers,
    incident detail, vendor names, architecture talk.
17. What each participant cares about (C): their current pressures,
    their org's goals, their personal win conditions.
18. Relationship history (C): allies, skeptics, unknowns. Past
    friction gets a prepared posture, not a grudge.
19. Past commitments between participants (C): who owes whom what,
    from transcripts and threads — the most commonly dropped prep.
20. Power map (C/R): who actually decides, who influences, who
    attends without stake. Prep serves the decider first.
21. Attendees vs optional (C): optionals who show up signal interest;
    required people who skip signal the meeting's real priority.
22. Who prepares (T): some participants read pre-reads, some react
    live. Prep notes which kind each key person is.
23. Communication styles (T): direct vs diplomatic, data vs story,
    impatient vs expansive. Match the room's bandwidth.
24. Cultural and org norms (T): how this org disagrees, how it
    decides, what candor costs here.
25. Absent stakeholders (C/R): who is affected but not in the room,
    and what must not be decided without them.
26. New-joiner sensitivity (T): anyone in their first 90 days gets
    context, not jargon; anyone who just changed roles gets the
    new-role read.

## 3. The principal's position

27. Private goals for the meeting (C): what the principal wants out
    of the room, stated plainly and kept out of anything shared.
28. What's in it for them (C): how this meeting advances the
    principal's professional and personal goals — or the honest
    answer that it doesn't, and what to do about that.
29. How it benefits others (C): the principal's contribution framed
    as service to the room's goals, not self-promotion.
30. Stated vs real agenda (C/R): what the invite says vs what will
    actually be litigated. Prep names the delta.
31. Principal's energy and load (S/T): fifth meeting of the day gets
    shorter prep and explicit permission to be brief.
32. Preparation budget (S): minutes available before the meeting.
    The skill sizes the pack to the budget and says what was cut.
33. Principal's authority in the room (C): can they commit, decide,
    spend, hire, kill? Unclear authority is a risk to name.
34. Principal's blind spots for this topic (R): what they don't know
    that the room might assume they do — the dangerous quadrant.
35. Principal's known positions (C): what they have already said
    publicly, so the prep never contradicts the record.
36. Emotional register (T/R): fraught topics (layoffs, performance,
    conflict) need steadier language and explicit de-escalation
    lines; celebratory ones need genuine warmth, not hype.

## 4. Knowledge to pull

37. Workspace OKF knowledge base (C): the durable facts, decisions,
    and positions relevant to the topic.
38. Prior transcripts with these participants (C): what was said
    last time, verbatim where attribution matters.
39. Communications since (C): email, chat, and thread traffic
    between the participants — the meeting continues between
    meetings.
40. Tickets, commits, deliverables (C): the actual record of what
    shipped, for R4's mandatory accomplishments.
41. Docs and decks (C): proposals, specs, and slides the room has
    seen or will see.
42. Reader profiles (C/T): the per-person files from R12 — what
    they were told before, what landed.
43. Principal's own writing samples (T): 2–3 recent messages to a
    comparable reader, to match register.
44. External research (C): for new external people and new topics —
    background, public positions, news. Sourced, never guessed.
45. Competitive and market context (C): for strategy and
    partnership meetings, the outside world the room decides in.
46. Numbers that must reconcile (R): any figure the principal
    might cite must be re-verified against its source the same
    day — stale numbers are the commonest prep failure.

## 5. Risk

47. Disclosure boundary (R): what must not leave the room — trade
    secrets, personnel matters, negotiable positions, others'
    private information. Externals redraw this line entirely.
48. Discoverability (R): anything written or recorded may be read
    back later. Self-assessments use counsel register: gaps as
    distance-to-a-standard, no legal conclusions, mechanics
    neutral.
49. Topics to avoid (R): the don't-raise footer from R1 — only
    plausible mistakes earn a line.
50. What could go wrong (R): the three likeliest bad turns and a
    prepared line for each. Not pessimism — readiness.
51. Traps and ambushes (R): known hard questions, hostile framings,
    decisions smuggled into status meetings. Name them.
52. Commitments the principal must not make (R): authority limits,
    budget, dates, headcount — the lines to hold live.
53. Prior leaks and sensitivities (R): what this room already knows
    that it shouldn't, and what that implies for candor.
54. Principal's tells under pressure (R): hedging, over-explaining,
    volunteering defects (R9). Prep names the specific guard.
55. No-surprises rule (R): anyone who will be surprised by an
    outcome in the room should have been pre-briefed. Prep flags
    who still needs a call before the meeting.
56. Exit conditions (R): what "good enough" looks like if the
    meeting goes sideways — the minimum viable outcome.

## 6. Output shaping

57. Length target (S): one page per 30 minutes of 1:1 is the
    default; forums get a page and a half; standups get five
    lines. The pack states its sizing in one line.
58. Structure variant (S): 1:1, small room, forum, external, and
    interview shapes — see structures.md. Sections that don't
    apply are dropped, never filled.
59. During-meeting usability (S): scannable in under five seconds
    per lookup; the decision or ask is the most findable line.
60. Capture surface (C): every prep ships with a capture half —
    what to note live (decisions, owners, dates, quotes) so the
    debrief has raw material.
61. Basis statement (C): the sources read and the newest source's
    date, per R10. Thin basis is declared, not hidden.
62. Confidence marks (R): facts vs inferences vs guesses, marked
    inline. A guess presented as a fact is a prep failure.
63. Drivers note (C): which factors above shaped this pack, in two
    lines. The principal should see the reasoning, not just the
    result.
64. Expiry (S): prep decays. The pack states the meeting time it
    was built for; a reschedule older than the basis triggers a
    refresh, not a reuse.
