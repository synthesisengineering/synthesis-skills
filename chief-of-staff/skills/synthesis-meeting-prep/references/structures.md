# Meeting prep — structures

One shape with sections dropped, varying by meeting type. Sections
that do not apply are dropped rather than filled. Every structure
ends with the capture half and the don't-raise footer.

## 1:1 (report, peer, boss, skip)

1. Reader — one line: who, how technical, relationship, cares.
2. Open with — the framing sentence or two.
3. Credit — named people, specific contributions.
4. Where things stand — delivered, in the reader's register.
5. What I want them to know — strategic, 3–5 items.
6. Decisions or asks — what needs to happen in the room.
7. Ask them — questions, because a meeting is not a broadcast.
8. If asked — prepared short answers.
9. Footer — don't-raise list.

Adjustments: with a report, section 6 leads (their blocked items
first) and section 5 shrinks. With a boss, sections 4–5 merge into
judgement-and-decisions; activity detail drops. Skip-level: section
7 leads — listening posture.

## Small room (3–5, working meeting)

1. Room — one line each: who decides, who is new, remote attendees.
2. Objective — the one outcome this meeting must produce.
3. Context in brief — what the room needs shared to decide.
4. Options on the table — with the principal's read, plainly marked.
5. Credit — whose work made the options real.
6. Ask the room — the questions that unblock the decision.
7. If asked — prepared answers for the known hard questions.
8. Footer — don't-raise list.

## Forum (6+, review, exec, all-hands-adjacent)

1. Room — decider, influencers, new faces, remote; candor level.
2. Open with — one framing idea, no name-drops (R7c).
3. Where things stand — outcomes only, executive register.
4. What I want them to know — strategic, max 3.
5. The ask — exactly one, findable in five seconds.
6. If asked — short answers; numbers re-verified same-day.
7. Footer — don't-raise list (forums earn the longest footers).

Forums get no credit section in the pack — credit is given live, by
name, and the prep lists who and for what in one line under the ask.

## External (customer, vendor, partner, press-adjacent)

1. Counterparty — who, what they want, what they know, research basis.
2. Disclosure boundary — what must not leave the room, stated first.
3. Objective — the principal's private goal plus the shared goal.
4. Where things stand — outcomes safe for this audience.
5. What I want them to know — max 3, relationship-serving.
6. Asks and offers — what to request, what to give.
7. If asked — answers inside the boundary; deflections for outside.
8. Footer — don't-raise list (externals redraw it entirely).

## Interview (hiring, diligence, press)

1. Subject — who, role, what is known, research basis.
2. Objective — the hiring / diligence / story question to answer.
3. What I want to learn — max 5, ordered by importance.
4. Questions — open, specific, sequenced; follow-ups drafted.
5. Signals to watch — what good and bad look like for this role.
6. Sell points — why them, why us, why now (hiring only).
7. Footer — off-limits topics (legal and human).

## Standup / sync (15 minutes or less)

Five lines: what I finished · what I'm on · what I'm blocked on ·
what I need from the room · what I'm not raising (one line).

## Capture half (every structure)

Appended to every prep, filled live or from the transcript:

- Decisions — what was decided, by whom, verbatim where it matters.
- Commitments — owner, deliverable, date. No ownerless commits.
- Quotes — lines worth keeping exactly.
- Prep-vs-reality — what the prep missed; feed to reader profiles.
- Follow-ups — drafted, not just listed.
